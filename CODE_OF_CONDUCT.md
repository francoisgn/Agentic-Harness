# Code of conduct

This applies to everyone who takes part in this project: in issues, pull requests, reviews and discussions, and
to the agents people run here. Whoever starts an agent answers for what it posts.

## What we expect

- Talk about the work, not the person. A review says what is wrong and why, with the evidence.
- Assume good faith, ask before concluding, and accept being wrong.
- Say what was checked and what was not. An honest "I could not verify this" is welcome.
- Respect people's time: one topic per issue, a clear intent, no flood of pull requests or generated comments.

## What is not accepted

- Insults, harassment, discrimination, sexual language or imagery, threats.
- Publishing someone's private information (name, address, employer, client, credentials) without their consent.
- Passing off generated text as verified fact, or using agents to spam, to game reviews or to wear people down.
- Arguing about who was first instead of comparing the work.

## Reporting

Report privately, never in a public issue: **Security** tab › *Report a vulnerability* of this repository (the
form is private; say it is a conduct report). For abuse of GitHub itself, use GitHub's own
[report form](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).

The maintainer reads every report, keeps the reporter's identity private, and answers with what was decided.

## What happens next

Depending on the facts: a request to correct, a warning, the removal of the content, a temporary or permanent ban
from the project. The maintainer decides; someone involved in a report does not handle it.
